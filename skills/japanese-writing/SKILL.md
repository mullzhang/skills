# Japanese Writing

Apply these rules to agent-authored Japanese prose in explanations, summaries, reports, reviews, and similar documents.

## Required Reference

Before starting, read [references/japanese-tech-writing.md](references/japanese-tech-writing.md) in full and apply it as the base writing standard.
The sections below add only rules not covered by that reference.

## Rule Precedence

When rules conflict, use this order:

1. Preserve fixed headings, field names, enumerated values, identifiers, and notation required by the user or target format.
2. Preserve the source's claims, terminology, quotations, and bibliographic data.
3. Write all other prose in natural Japanese.

Do not translate a fixed string defined in English.
Write the surrounding explanation in Japanese.
Change source text only within the scope of an explicit request to translate, summarize, or paraphrase it.

## State Meaning in Japanese

- Do not substitute an English word for an explanation.
- Do not connect English words using only Japanese particles.
  Rewrite the entire expression in idiomatic Japanese.
  Examples:
  - Bad: `sourceをingestする`
    Good: `原資料を取り込む`
  - Bad: `conceptをupdateする`
    Good: `概念ページを更新する`
  - Bad: `contextをshareする`
    Good: `文脈を共有する`
  - Bad: `sourceからinsightをextractする`
    Good: `原資料から知見を取り出す`
- Do not compress meaning into short English words such as `bridge`, `fork`, `merge`, `agency`, `extractive`, `context`, `insight`, or `implication`.
  Choose a Japanese expression that conveys the intended meaning in context, such as `橋渡し`, `分岐`, `統合`, `主体性`, `収奪的`, `文脈`, `知見`, or `示唆`.
- When translating a technical term would distort it, explain the term in Japanese first and add the original term in parentheses once only when needed for search or verification.
- Do not repeatedly pair the Japanese translation with the original term in the same paragraph.
- Keep loanwords that are established as the natural Japanese choice.
  Their existence does not justify retaining the original English spelling.
- Do not perform word-for-word replacement.
  Rebuild word order, dependencies, and subject-predicate relationships as natural Japanese.

## Permitted English

Keep the following in English when accuracy or reference integrity requires it:

- Fixed headings, field names, enumerated values, operation names, and notation defined by the user, template, or schema
- File paths, filenames, identifiers, link targets, URLs, commands, code, and regular expressions
- Official product, organization, paper, and book names
- Source quotations and source-defined central terms
- Established abbreviations such as AI, LLM, API, PDF, DOI, URL, YAML, CI/CD, and RAG

These exceptions do not permit English shorthand in explanatory prose.
Add a Japanese explanation when an abbreviation or original term is not self-explanatory.

## Separate Source Text from Authored Prose

- Do not translate or paraphrase quotations, bibliographic data, or source text that must be preserved unless the user asks for it.
- Clearly distinguish source text from agent-authored summaries, annotations, and evaluations.
- Do not carry English phrasing from the source into agent-authored prose without deciding that it is necessary.

## Final Check

1. Confirm that fixed strings, identifiers, quotations, and bibliographic data have not changed.
2. Search agent-authored prose for Latin-script words.
3. Exclude code, paths, links, official names, quotations, and other permitted cases, then judge every remaining English term individually.
4. Rewrite unnecessary English by rereading the whole sentence and restoring natural Japanese meaning and order.
5. Confirm that every retained abbreviation or original term is understandable without an omitted Japanese explanation.
